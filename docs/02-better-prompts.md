# 02 · Better prompts

The prompt optimizer turns a rough request into a strong one. You can type the way you talk.
It does the structuring.

## How to use it

Say any of these, then paste or type your request:
- "Improve this prompt: …"
- "Make this better: …"
- "Make this more ambitious: …"
- Or just paste a long, messy request and say "turn this into a proper prompt".

You get back a ready-to-paste prompt, then the question: "Run it now, or copy it into a new chat?"

## What a strong prompt has

| Line | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | What should be true afterwards | "More people book the call" |
| **Task** | The thing to do, written as an action | "Get a quiet lead to book the call, in one short email" |
| **Context** | Who it's for and why | "Small business owners who downloaded my guide" |
| **Inputs** | What Claude should work from, rawest first | "My full sales call notes, not my summary" |
| **Methods and tools** | What a top professional would actually use | "One reader, one problem, one ask" |
| **Output format** | The exact shape | "3 subject lines, 100 words, one link" |
| **Constraints** | Must-do and must-avoid | "Casual. No hype words." |
| **Stretch target** | Optional: a goal that sounds too big | "Make it the best email in their inbox this week" |
| **Who checks it** | Something other than Claude itself | "A fresh chat with a checklist, then me" |
| **Model and effort** | Filled in by the model picker | "Opus, medium" |

Quick jobs only get the lines they need. Important jobs get all of them.

## The three questions that make it ambitious

On important jobs, the kit asks itself three questions before writing your prompt:

1. **Aim higher:** what is the result behind this request?
   "Write slides" becomes "win the client".
2. **Go deeper:** how would a top professional do it, from which original material?
   "Analyse my sales" becomes "work from the full export, test my theory about why sales dipped in March, and show me the pattern I'd least expect".
3. **Go wider:** which other fields would help?
   "Write a pricing page" becomes "help a nervous first-timer feel safe enough to sign up", with a beginner coach, a designer and a skeptical buyer's point of view.

## Two prompts to keep handy

Paste either one after any request to push it further.

**Go deeper**
```
Hold on before you start. Give me three short lists:
1. What a top professional would add to this that I left out.
2. The original material they'd want from me (files, data, examples), not a summary.
3. How we'll prove the result works, using something other than your opinion.
Then show me an improved version of my request that includes all three, and wait for my OK.
```

**Expert team**
```
Put together a small team for this: four or five specialists from different fields, plus two
critics who only hunt for weaknesses. For each person, write one line on the checks or methods
they'd bring. Then do the work applying every one of those methods, and finish with the critics'
top objections and how you dealt with each. Use their skills; don't imitate their voices.
```

## Common mistakes the kit fixes

- **"You are a world-class copywriter…"** Job titles change the tone, not the work. The kit names the methods instead.
- **No goal.** The kit adds the outcome, so Claude knows what "good" means.
- **Letting Claude grade itself.** The kit always names an outside check on important work.
- **Summaries instead of sources.** The kit asks for the original material.
