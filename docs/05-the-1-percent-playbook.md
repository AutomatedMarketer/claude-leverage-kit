# 05 · The 1% playbook

Everyone has access to the same AI. What separates people who get extraordinary results isn't
a secret tool. It's how they ask, and what they ask for.

## The four levels of using AI

| Level | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Faster** | The same task in less time | "Write this email" |
| **2. Aim higher** | Ask for the result, not just the task | "Get this lead to book a call" |
| **3. Go deeper** | Ask for the professional's way: their methods, the original data, a bold target | "Use the full order export, test my theory about refunds, and show me what I'd least expect" |
| **4. Go wider** | Bring in other fields and critics | "Have a designer, a first-time buyer and two critics shape and attack this page" |

Level 1 is where everyone starts. Each level above it widens the gap between ordinary and
outstanding results.

## Habit 1: name the result behind the request

Before you ask for anything, ask yourself: **when this is finished, what will be different?**
Then tell Claude that, too.

- Not "make me a pitch deck". Instead: "help me win this client. Here's what they care about."
- Not "fix this error". Instead: "make sure customers can check out without problems".

If you ask for the same thing every week, that's your signal. Ask Claude once to make it a
repeatable workflow.

## Habit 2: ask for methods, not job titles

"You are a world-class marketer" changes the tone, not the work. Ask instead:
**"Which methods would a top professional use here? List them, then use them."**
You don't have to know the methods yourself. Make Claude spell them out first.

## Habit 3: hand over the original material

A summary is your opinion of the data, with details already removed. Give Claude the full
spreadsheet, the whole transcript, the original reviews. Surprises hide in the details you'd
have cut.

## Habit 4: set a bold target next to the safe one

Ask for the safe version and the bold version. "Faster" becomes "loads in under one second".
"A good email" becomes "the email they forward to a colleague". If Claude pushes back, ask what
would have to be true to hit the bold target, and try that.

## Habit 5: build a team, with critics

For work that matters, ask Claude to pick specialists from different fields, and two critics
whose only job is finding weaknesses. Ask it to apply their checks and methods, not to imitate
their voices.

**Trick:** describe what the work must *do*, not what it *is*. "A pricing page" is a thing.
"Help a nervous first-timer feel safe enough to sign up" is a job, and it tells you which
specialists to bring in.

## Habit 6: decide who checks the work before you start

AI tends to agree with you, and it rates its own work generously. For anything important,
choose the check **before you begin**:
- a tool (a test, a page-speed check, a spell checker),
- a fresh chat that only sees the result and your checklist,
- or you.

## Habit 7: use the plan you already pay for

Check your usage the day before your weekly reset. Spend what's left on your most ambitious
work: the team review, the deep check, the workflow that saves you time every week after.

## The real limit

The tools get more capable every few weeks. The bottleneck is rarely the AI anymore. It's what
we think to ask it for. Ask bigger, give it better material, and check the result properly.

**AI is leverage. Learn to use it.**
