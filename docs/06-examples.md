# 06 · Examples

Real business jobs, before and after. Copy any "after" prompt and change the details.

## 1. Client proposal

**Before:** "Write a proposal for a website redesign for a dental clinic."

**After (aim higher + go deeper):**
```
Outcome: the clinic owner says yes on our next call.
Task: help a busy clinic owner believe a new website will bring more bookings, and decide in one read.
Inputs: their current site (link), our discovery call notes (attached, full), our price list.
Methods and tools: what a top agency strategist would do — quick audit of their current site,
two competitor sites for contrast, one clear before/after, a simple return-on-investment estimate.
Output format: 1-page proposal, 3 options (good / better / best), one recommended.
Constraints: plain language, no jargon, honest numbers only from the notes.
Who checks it: a fresh chat reads it as the clinic owner and lists every objection; then me.
Model and effort: Opus, medium.
```

## 2. Weekly report you write every Friday

**Before:** "Summarise this week's sales numbers."

**After (aim higher → make it repeatable):**
```
Outcome: I stop writing this report by hand every week.
Task: create a reusable checklist and template that turns my weekly sales export into the report,
then run it on this week's export as the first test.
Inputs: this week's full sales export (attached), last month's four reports as examples.
Output format: the template, the checklist, and this week's finished report.
Who checks it: compare this week's totals against the raw export line by line.
Model and effort: Opus, medium.
```

## 3. Sales page before launch (go wider)

**Before:** "Make my sales page better."

**After:**
```
Put together a small team for this sales page: four or five specialists from different fields,
plus two critics who only hunt for weaknesses. For each person, write one line on the checks or
methods they'd bring. Then review the page applying every one of those methods, and give me a
prioritised list of changes, most important first.
Page: (paste the page text or link)
Who checks it: every critic objection is either fixed or marked "won't fix" with a reason.
Model and effort: Opus leading, Sonnet helpers. Ask me before starting the team.
```

## 4. Why are refunds going up? (go deeper)

**Before:** "Why are we getting so many refunds?"

**After:**
```
Outcome: we know the real top reasons for refunds and can act on the biggest one this month.
Task: test our theory that most refunds come from late deliveries, using the full order data.
Inputs: the full order export with order dates, ship dates, products and refund reasons (attached).
Methods and tools: what an operations analyst would do — compare refunded and kept orders by
product, shipping time and supplier; read every written refund reason.
Stretch target: name one fix that would cut refunds by a quarter, with the numbers behind it.
Who checks it: every claim cites the rows it came from; I spot-check five.
Model and effort: Opus, high.
```

## 5. Gym membership page (say what it must do)

**Before:** "Write a pricing page for my gym."

**After:**
```
Outcome: more first-timers start a trial membership.
Task: help a nervous first-timer feel safe enough to sign up for a trial.
Inputs: our three plans and prices, 10 real member reviews, photos of the beginner classes.
Methods and tools: how a beginner coach answers first-day fears, how a conversion designer lays
out a pricing table, how a skeptical buyer reads the small print.
Output format: headline, the three plans side by side, a "your first week" section, FAQ, one button.
Constraints: only facts from our inputs; no invented results or testimonials.
Who checks it: a fresh chat reads it as a nervous beginner and lists every doubt left unanswered.
Model and effort: Opus, medium.
```

## 6. A quick job (don't overdo it)

**Before:** "Fix the grammar in this paragraph."

**After:** nothing. It's already clear. The kit skips the template for quick jobs, and so should you.
